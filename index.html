Global spatiotemporal distribution of adiposity-associated disease risk, 2015–2050

Interactive results explorer — BMI and disease burden attributable to high BMI

Study overview

Overweight and obesity continue to rise worldwide, reshaping the global burden of chronic disease. This study quantifies the global, spatiotemporally explicit distribution of disease burden attributable to high body-mass index (BMI), for the historical period 2015–2021 and projections to 2050 under four Shared Socioeconomic Pathways (SSP1-2.6, SSP2-4.5, SSP3-7.0, SSP5-8.5).

What this explorer shows

  • BMI exposure — mean BMI and obesity prevalence (BMI ≥ 30) at 0.1° grid resolution, 2015–2050, for every country, sex, and five-year age group.
  • Attributable disease burden — deaths and DALYs attributable to high BMI for 23 headline causes (12 cancers, cardiovascular diseases, metabolic and other conditions) plus their aggregate, mapped at 0.1° resolution and charted as national and global time series, by sex and age.

Headline findings

  • Global mean adult BMI rises from 25.0 kg/m² (2015) to 27.7 kg/m² (2050, SSP2-4.5); global obesity prevalence nearly doubles, from 14.4% to ~30.7%.
  • High-BMI-attributable deaths and DALYs increase in all four scenarios; differences between scenarios at the burden level are small because disease baselines are shared across scenarios.
  • Attributable burden is increasingly concentrated in low- and middle-income regions, with marked spatial heterogeneity at sub-national scale.

How to use

Use the BMI tab to explore exposure (mean BMI / obesity) and the Disease burden tab to explore attributable deaths and DALYs by disease, measure, year and sex. Click any country on the map to view its trend; use the year slider to move through 2015–2050. See the Methods tab for the analysis pipeline and replication code.

Maps show overall (both sexes, all ages) SSP2-4.5; sex/age selections apply to the trend chart. Burden maps for 2030–2045 interpolate the spatial pattern between 2025 and 2050 (national totals exact).

Methods

Analysis pipeline

  1. Inputs. GBD 2021 total and high-BMI-attributable deaths/DALYs (65 causes, 2015–2021), GBD Foresight reference burden (2022–2050), GBD adult overweight/obesity forecast (1990–2050), NCD-RisC national BMI distributions, IIASA SSP / WiC demographics, CMIP6 climate, ERA5, ISIMIP/WorldPop population grids, GHS urbanicity, Natural Earth boundaries, WCRF/AICR dose–response functions.
  2. Covariate harmonisation. All future drivers are harmonised at national and 0.5°/0.1° grid scale across four SSPs (population, GDP, urbanicity, climate, PM2.5, night-time lights, built-up surface).
  3. BMI modelling. National BMI-7-category anchors (2025–2050, 204 territories) are combined with a future-capable multilevel regression with poststratification (MRP) model (Bayesian, Stan/CmdStan) and produced as annual 0.1° BMI-category probability grids for 2025–2050; the historical layer (2015–2024) uses the same framework fitted to NCD-RisC data, with temporal backtesting.
  4. Attribution. Cause-specific population-attributable fractions (PAFs) are computed from BMI-category probabilities and published relative risks (WCRF/AICR for colorectal cancer; GBD crosswalk for the remaining causes), then applied to GBD 2021 (historical) and GBD Foresight (projected) reference burdens to obtain attributable deaths and DALYs.
  5. Spatialisation. National attributable burdens are allocated to 0.1° grid cells with population × PAF weights and validated against GBD country totals; spatial interpolation is verified at national level.
  6. Outputs. National time series (overall, sex, age group) and 0.1° maps for 23 headline causes plus their aggregate; main and supplementary manuscript figures; this interactive explorer.

Notes on interpretation

  • Historical estimates (2015–2021) derive from GBD 2021; projections (2025–2050) apply study PAFs to the shared GBD Foresight reference burden. The two segments differ slightly in level for some countries; the displayed transition is smoothed over four years for readability only.
  • Scenario differences in attributable burden are small by design: the reference burden is common across SSPs, so differences enter only through BMI exposure.
  • Burden maps for 2030–2045 interpolate the spatial pattern between 2025 and 2050 weight rasters; national totals are exact.

Methodological validation

About the historical–projection transition

The historical series (2015–2021) and the projection series (2025–2050) are anchored to two different source datasets: historical exposure uses the MRP reconstruction fitted to observed NCD-RisC national BMI distributions, while projections are anchored to the GBD adult overweight/obesity forecast (1990–2050). For some countries the two products differ systematically in level (median absolute gap ≈ 3.7 percentage points of obesity prevalence; global aggregate ≈ 1.8 pp). This is a known property of the two public data sources, not a modelling error; a dedicated bridge sensitivity analysis quantifies it.

On this website, the junction is blended over an eight-year transition window for readability; underlying values are unchanged — the smoothing is display-only and the replicated pipeline reproduces the unsmoothed series exactly.

Accuracy assurance

  • Benchmark against GBD 2021: study attributable burdens reproduce official GBD high-BMI estimates at national level for 2015–2021.
  • Mass conservation: 0.1° burden grids sum to their national totals within 0.002% (e.g., CRC 2015 grid sum = 99.998% of the national figure).
  • Model validation: the MRP model passes temporal backtesting, source holdout, and the full-covariate non-inferiority gate.
  • Data gates: covariate harmonisation (Gate B/C) and multiscale delivery QA pass before any downstream computation.
  • Replication: every figure and table in this explorer is regenerated by the pipeline in the linked repository.

Coverage note

Historical BMI maps (2015/2020) cover fewer grid cells than the projection maps because the historical MRP layer is restricted to areas with sufficient training support; projection maps (2025–2050) are full-coverage. This is expected and does not affect national or global totals, which are computed from the national anchors.
